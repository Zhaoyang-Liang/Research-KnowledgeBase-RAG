## Section Draft: Core Factorization

### 1. Scope and Normalized Model

This section proves the algebraic core of our construction. We work at the message-coordinate level and ignore ciphertext noise, key-switching error, rescaling error, and implementation-specific scaling. These effects are orthogonal to the algebraic factorization and will be accounted for in the parameter and implementation sections.

Throughout this section, all vector spaces are over \(\mathbb C\). We use a normalized logical layout. Concrete implementations may use bit-reversal, blocked layouts, half-slot conventions, real/imaginary branches, and library-specific scaling conventions. In the theorem below, layout maps are allowed to contain coordinate permutations and reorderings only. Any non-permutation scaling must be normalized away and absorbed into the definitions of \(\mathsf{C2S}\), \(\mathsf{S2C}\), and the scalar map used by \(\mathsf{EvalMod}\). Arbitrary diagonal rescalings are not part of the abstract theorem, because they do not in general commute with a nonlinear component-wise map.

The purpose of this section is to prove:

\[
\mathsf{S2C}_N \circ \mathsf{EvalMod}_N \circ \mathsf{C2S}_N
=
\mathsf{Merge}_k
\circ
\left(
\bigoplus_{r=0}^{k-1}
\mathsf{S2C}_n \circ \mathsf{EvalMod}_n \circ \mathsf{C2S}_n
\right)
\circ
\mathsf{Split}_k,
\]

under the ideal normalized coefficient-split semantics, where \(N=kn\).

This theorem should not be read as saying that coefficient split preserves the original large-ring canonical slots. It does not. The statement is instead about the coordinates after \(\mathsf{C2S}\): after \(\mathsf{C2S}\), the relevant data are coefficient-packed coordinates, and these coordinates are exactly decomposed by residue class.

### 2. Notation

Fix positive integers \(N=kn\). Let

\[
\mathcal R_N = \mathbb C[X]/(X^N+1),
\qquad
\mathcal R_n = \mathbb C[Y]/(Y^n+1),
\]

and identify \(\mathcal R_n\) with the subalgebra of \(\mathcal R_N\) generated by \(X^k\), via \(Y\mapsto X^k\). This map is well-defined because

\[
(X^k)^n=X^{kn}=X^N=-1
\]

in \(\mathcal R_N\). It is injective: the elements

\[
1,X^k,X^{2k},\ldots,X^{(n-1)k}
\]

are linearly independent in the monomial basis of \(\mathcal R_N\), so no relation of degree \(<n\) is introduced beyond \(Y^n+1=0\).

In this section, \(d\) denotes the full complex embedding dimension of \(\mathbb C[X]/(X^d+1)\). This is not the number of user-visible CKKS complex slots under the usual half-slot convention. The half-slot CKKS model can be recovered by restricting to the conjugate-compatible subspace and applying fixed projection and layout maps. We use the full model because it makes the algebraic factorization transparent.

For \(d\in\{N,n\}\), define two coordinate spaces:

\[
\mathsf{Can}_d
\]

for canonical-slot coordinates, and

\[
\mathsf{Coeff}_d
\]

for coefficient-packed coordinates. If \(P\in \mathcal R_d\), we write

\[
\operatorname{can}_d(P)\in \mathsf{Can}_d
\]

for its canonical-slot vector, and

\[
\operatorname{coeff}_d(P)\in \mathsf{Coeff}_d
\]

for its coefficient vector in the monomial basis.

Both

\[
\operatorname{can}_d:\mathcal R_d\to \mathsf{Can}_d
\]

and

\[
\operatorname{coeff}_d:\mathcal R_d\to \mathsf{Coeff}_d
\]

are fixed linear coordinate isomorphisms.

We model the ideal bootstrapping linear transforms as maps

\[
\mathsf{C2S}_d:\mathsf{Can}_d\to \mathsf{Coeff}_d,
\qquad
\mathsf{S2C}_d:\mathsf{Coeff}_d\to \mathsf{Can}_d,
\]

with the semantic properties

\[
\mathsf{C2S}_d(\operatorname{can}_d(P))
=
\operatorname{coeff}_d(P),
\]

and

\[
\mathsf{S2C}_d(\operatorname{coeff}_d(P))
=
\operatorname{can}_d(P).
\]

Equivalently,

\[
\mathsf{C2S}_d
=
\operatorname{coeff}_d\circ \operatorname{can}_d^{-1},
\qquad
\mathsf{S2C}_d
=
\operatorname{can}_d\circ \operatorname{coeff}_d^{-1}.
\]

In a full canonical embedding model, one may view \(\mathsf{S2C}_d\) as an evaluation/Vandermonde map and \(\mathsf{C2S}_d\) as its inverse. We will not need this matrix-level description; only the above semantic relations are used.

For any large-ring coefficient vector

\[
p=(a_0,\ldots,a_{N-1})^T\in \mathsf{Coeff}_N,
\]

define the residue-class selection map

\[
S_r:\mathsf{Coeff}_N\to \mathsf{Coeff}_n
\]

by

\[
S_r p
=
(a_r,a_{r+k},a_{r+2k},\ldots,a_{r+(n-1)k})^T,
\qquad r=0,\ldots,k-1.
\]

Let

\[
\Pi_k:\mathsf{Coeff}_N\to \bigoplus_{r=0}^{k-1}\mathsf{Coeff}_n
\]

be the coefficient layout map

\[
\Pi_k p = (S_0p,\ldots,S_{k-1}p).
\]

In the normalized layout, \(\Pi_k\) is exactly residue-class grouping. In an implementation, \(\Pi_k\) may include fixed permutations or block orderings. It must not include arbitrary coordinate scalings in the theorem below, unless those scalings have already been normalized into the definitions of the surrounding maps and of the scalar EvalMod function.

For \(P(X)\in \mathcal R_N\), define the coefficient/module split by the unique decomposition

\[
P(X)=\sum_{r=0}^{k-1}X^rP_r(X^k),
\qquad
P_r(Y)\in \mathcal R_n.
\]

Equivalently, if \(P(X)=\sum_{i=0}^{N-1}a_iX^i\), then

\[
P_r(Y)=\sum_{j=0}^{n-1}a_{r+jk}Y^j.
\]

This induces a canonical-coordinate map

\[
\mathsf{Split}_k:\mathsf{Can}_N\to \bigoplus_{r=0}^{k-1}\mathsf{Can}_n
\]

defined by

\[
\mathsf{Split}_k(\operatorname{can}_N(P))
=
(\operatorname{can}_n(P_0),\ldots,\operatorname{can}_n(P_{k-1})).
\]

The merge map

\[
\mathsf{Merge}_k:\bigoplus_{r=0}^{k-1}\mathsf{Can}_n\to \mathsf{Can}_N
\]

is induced by the inverse assembly

\[
(P_0,\ldots,P_{k-1})
\mapsto
\sum_{r=0}^{k-1}X^rP_r(X^k).
\]

Thus

\[
\mathsf{Merge}_k(\operatorname{can}_n(P_0),\ldots,\operatorname{can}_n(P_{k-1}))
=
\operatorname{can}_N\left(\sum_{r=0}^{k-1}X^rP_r(X^k)\right).
\]

### 3. Abstract Split-Compatible Sandwich Theorem

We first prove an abstract theorem. It explains why the CKKS bootstrapping core factorization is not an accident of CKKS notation, but a general property of a split-compatible sandwich:

\[
\text{linear map}
\quad\to\quad
\text{component-wise map}
\quad\to\quad
\text{linear map}.
\]

#### Theorem 1: Split-Compatible Sandwich Theorem

Let \(V_N,W_N,V_n,W_n\) be vector spaces. Let

\[
L_N^{(1)}:V_N\to W_N,
\qquad
L_n^{(1)}:V_n\to W_n,
\]

and

\[
L_N^{(2)}:W_N\to V_N,
\qquad
L_n^{(2)}:W_n\to V_n
\]

be linear maps. Let

\[
\Phi_N:W_N\to W_N,
\qquad
\Phi_n:W_n\to W_n
\]

be arbitrary maps, not necessarily linear.

For a possibly nonlinear map \(\Phi_n\), the notation

\[
\bigoplus_{r=0}^{k-1}\Phi_n
\]

means the component-wise product map

\[
(w_0,\ldots,w_{k-1})
\mapsto
(\Phi_n(w_0),\ldots,\Phi_n(w_{k-1})).
\]

Thus no linearity of \(\Phi_n\) is implied by the direct-sum notation.

Assume there exist maps

\[
\mathsf{Split}:V_N\to \bigoplus_{r=0}^{k-1}V_n,
\qquad
\mathsf{Merge}:\bigoplus_{r=0}^{k-1}V_n\to V_N,
\]

and a layout map

\[
\Pi:W_N\to \bigoplus_{r=0}^{k-1}W_n
\]

such that the following three compatibility conditions hold:

\[
\left(\bigoplus_{r=0}^{k-1}L_n^{(1)}\right)\circ \mathsf{Split}
=
\Pi\circ L_N^{(1)},
\tag{1}
\]

\[
\left(\bigoplus_{r=0}^{k-1}\Phi_n\right)\circ \Pi
=
\Pi\circ \Phi_N,
\tag{2}
\]

and

\[
\mathsf{Merge}\circ
\left(\bigoplus_{r=0}^{k-1}L_n^{(2)}\right)
\circ \Pi
=
L_N^{(2)}.
\tag{3}
\]

Define

\[
F_N=L_N^{(2)}\circ \Phi_N\circ L_N^{(1)}
\]

and

\[
F_n=L_n^{(2)}\circ \Phi_n\circ L_n^{(1)}.
\]

Then

\[
F_N
=
\mathsf{Merge}\circ
\left(\bigoplus_{r=0}^{k-1}F_n\right)
\circ
\mathsf{Split}.
\]

#### Proof

Starting from the right-hand side, expand the direct sum of the leaf maps:

\[
\begin{aligned}
&\mathsf{Merge}\circ
\left(\bigoplus_{r=0}^{k-1}F_n\right)
\circ
\mathsf{Split}
\\
&=
\mathsf{Merge}\circ
\left(\bigoplus_{r=0}^{k-1}
L_n^{(2)}\circ \Phi_n\circ L_n^{(1)}
\right)
\circ
\mathsf{Split}
\\
&=
\mathsf{Merge}\circ
\left(\bigoplus_{r=0}^{k-1}L_n^{(2)}\right)
\circ
\left(\bigoplus_{r=0}^{k-1}\Phi_n\right)
\circ
\left(\bigoplus_{r=0}^{k-1}L_n^{(1)}\right)
\circ
\mathsf{Split}.
\end{aligned}
\]

Using the first compatibility condition (1), this becomes

\[
\mathsf{Merge}\circ
\left(\bigoplus_{r=0}^{k-1}L_n^{(2)}\right)
\circ
\left(\bigoplus_{r=0}^{k-1}\Phi_n\right)
\circ
\Pi
\circ
L_N^{(1)}.
\]

Using the second compatibility condition (2), we get

\[
\mathsf{Merge}\circ
\left(\bigoplus_{r=0}^{k-1}L_n^{(2)}\right)
\circ
\Pi
\circ
\Phi_N
\circ
L_N^{(1)}.
\]

Finally, using the third compatibility condition (3), this is equal to

\[
L_N^{(2)}\circ \Phi_N\circ L_N^{(1)}
=
F_N.
\]

This proves the theorem. \(\square\)

#### Remark 1

The theorem does not require \(\Phi_N\) or \(\Phi_n\) to be linear. The only requirement is the compatibility condition (2). This is essential for CKKS bootstrapping, because \(\mathsf{EvalMod}\) is nonlinear but component-wise in the normalized model.

### 4. Component-Wise Maps Commute with Residue Grouping

We now record the concrete condition that will be used for \(\mathsf{EvalMod}\).

#### Lemma 2: Component-Wise Compatibility

Let

\[
\Phi_N=f^{\oplus N}:\mathbb C^N\to \mathbb C^N
\]

and

\[
\Phi_n=f^{\oplus n}:\mathbb C^n\to \mathbb C^n
\]

for the same scalar function \(f:\mathbb C\to\mathbb C\). Let

\[
\Pi_k(x_0,\ldots,x_{N-1})
=
\left(
(x_{jk})_{j=0}^{n-1},
(x_{1+jk})_{j=0}^{n-1},
\ldots,
(x_{k-1+jk})_{j=0}^{n-1}
\right).
\]

Equivalently, \(\Pi_k\) is residue-class grouping, possibly followed by fixed coordinate permutations. It does not include coordinate-dependent scalings.

Then

\[
\left(\bigoplus_{r=0}^{k-1}\Phi_n\right)\circ \Pi_k
=
\Pi_k\circ \Phi_N.
\]

#### Proof

Let \(x=(x_0,\ldots,x_{N-1})\). The left-hand side first groups \(x\) by residue class and then applies \(f\) on each leaf coordinate. Thus its coordinate in leaf \(r\) and position \(j\) is

\[
f(x_{r+jk}).
\]

The right-hand side first applies \(f\) to all \(N\) coordinates and then groups by residue class. Its coordinate in leaf \(r\) and position \(j\) is also

\[
f(x_{r+jk}).
\]

Since all coordinates agree, the two maps are identical. \(\square\)

#### Remark 2

This proof uses no additivity or multiplicativity of \(f\). It only uses that the same scalar function is applied independently to each coordinate, and that the layout operation is only a grouping/permutation. Therefore a nonlinear function such as the EvalMod approximation can commute with residue grouping, provided it does not mix different coordinates and no unnormalized diagonal scaling is placed across the nonlinear map.

### 5. Instantiating the Abstract Theorem for CKKS Bootstrapping

We now verify the three compatibility conditions of Theorem 1 for the conventional CKKS bootstrapping core.

The large-ring core is

\[
\mathsf{BTS}^{\mathrm{core}}_N
=
\mathsf{S2C}_N\circ
\mathsf{EvalMod}_N\circ
\mathsf{C2S}_N.
\]

The leaf core is

\[
\mathsf{BTS}^{\mathrm{core}}_n
=
\mathsf{S2C}_n\circ
\mathsf{EvalMod}_n\circ
\mathsf{C2S}_n.
\]

In the notation of Theorem 1, we set

\[
V_N=\mathsf{Can}_N,
\qquad
W_N=\mathsf{Coeff}_N,
\]

\[
V_n=\mathsf{Can}_n,
\qquad
W_n=\mathsf{Coeff}_n,
\]

and

\[
L_N^{(1)}=\mathsf{C2S}_N,
\qquad
L_n^{(1)}=\mathsf{C2S}_n,
\]

\[
L_N^{(2)}=\mathsf{S2C}_N,
\qquad
L_n^{(2)}=\mathsf{S2C}_n,
\]

\[
\Phi_N=\mathsf{EvalMod}_N,
\qquad
\Phi_n=\mathsf{EvalMod}_n.
\]

The split, merge, and layout maps are respectively

\[
\mathsf{Split}_k,\qquad \mathsf{Merge}_k,\qquad \Pi_k.
\]

It remains to prove the three compatibility conditions.

### 6. Split-C2S Compatibility

#### Lemma 3: Split-C2S Compatibility

Under the normalized layout convention,

\[
\left(\bigoplus_{r=0}^{k-1}\mathsf{C2S}_n\right)
\circ
\mathsf{Split}_k
=
\Pi_k
\circ
\mathsf{C2S}_N.
\]

#### Proof

Let

\[
P(X)=\sum_{i=0}^{N-1}a_iX^i\in \mathcal R_N,
\]

and write

\[
z=\operatorname{can}_N(P),
\qquad
p=\operatorname{coeff}_N(P)=(a_0,\ldots,a_{N-1})^T.
\]

By definition of the coefficient/module split,

\[
P(X)=\sum_{r=0}^{k-1}X^rP_r(X^k),
\]

where

\[
P_r(Y)=\sum_{j=0}^{n-1}a_{r+jk}Y^j.
\]

Therefore the coefficient vector of \(P_r\) is exactly

\[
\operatorname{coeff}_n(P_r)=S_rp.
\]

Consider the left-hand side. Applying \(\mathsf{Split}_k\) to \(z\) gives

\[
\mathsf{Split}_k(z)
=
(\operatorname{can}_n(P_0),\ldots,\operatorname{can}_n(P_{k-1})).
\]

Applying \(\mathsf{C2S}_n\) to each component gives

\[
\begin{aligned}
\left(
\bigoplus_{r=0}^{k-1}\mathsf{C2S}_n
\right)
\mathsf{Split}_k(z)
&=
(\operatorname{coeff}_n(P_0),\ldots,\operatorname{coeff}_n(P_{k-1}))
\\
&=
(S_0p,\ldots,S_{k-1}p)
\\
&=
\Pi_k p.
\end{aligned}
\]

Consider the right-hand side. Since \(\mathsf{C2S}_N(z)=p\), we have

\[
(\Pi_k\circ \mathsf{C2S}_N)(z)
=
\Pi_k p.
\]

Thus both sides agree on every \(z=\operatorname{can}_N(P)\). Since \(\operatorname{can}_N:\mathcal R_N\to\mathsf{Can}_N\) is a linear coordinate isomorphism, every element of \(\mathsf{Can}_N\) is uniquely of this form. Hence the two maps agree on the entire domain and are equal. \(\square\)

#### Interpretation

This lemma does not say that \(\mathsf{Split}_k\) preserves the original large-ring slots. It says that once each leaf applies \(\mathsf{C2S}_n\), the resulting leaf slots contain exactly the coefficient residue-class sub-vectors of the large-ring \(\mathsf{C2S}_N\) output.

### 7. EvalMod Compatibility

#### Lemma 4: EvalMod Compatibility

Assume that, after the normalized \(\mathsf{C2S}\) output scaling, the large-ring and leaf-ring EvalMod stages apply exactly the same scalar map to each coefficient-packed coordinate:

\[
\mathsf{EvalMod}_N=f^{\oplus N}
\]

and

\[
\mathsf{EvalMod}_n=f^{\oplus n}
\]

for one scalar approximation function \(f:\mathbb C\to\mathbb C\). In particular, the approximation polynomial, period normalization, input scaling, and output scaling are identical across the large and leaf cores. Then

\[
\left(\bigoplus_{r=0}^{k-1}\mathsf{EvalMod}_n\right)
\circ
\Pi_k
=
\Pi_k
\circ
\mathsf{EvalMod}_N.
\]

#### Proof

This is Lemma 2 with

\[
\Phi_N=\mathsf{EvalMod}_N,
\qquad
\Phi_n=\mathsf{EvalMod}_n.
\]

For completeness, let \(x=(x_0,\ldots,x_{N-1})\in \mathsf{Coeff}_N\). After grouping, the coordinate in leaf \(r\) and position \(j\) is \(x_{r+jk}\). Applying the leaf EvalMod gives

\[
f(x_{r+jk}).
\]

If we first apply the large-ring EvalMod, we obtain

\[
(f(x_0),\ldots,f(x_{N-1})).
\]

Grouping by \(\Pi_k\) gives the same coordinate

\[
f(x_{r+jk}).
\]

All coordinates are equal, so the maps are equal. \(\square\)

#### Interpretation

The nonlinearity of \(\mathsf{EvalMod}\) is not an obstruction. What matters is that \(\mathsf{EvalMod}\) acts component-wise on the coefficient-packed slots after \(\mathsf{C2S}\), using the same normalized scalar function on the large and leaf cores. A map that mixes neighboring or global coordinates, or a leaf EvalMod using a different normalization of \(f\), would not satisfy this lemma.

### 8. S2C-Merge Compatibility

#### Lemma 5: S2C-Merge Compatibility

Under the normalized layout convention,

\[
\mathsf{Merge}_k
\circ
\left(\bigoplus_{r=0}^{k-1}\mathsf{S2C}_n\right)
\circ
\Pi_k
=
\mathsf{S2C}_N.
\]

#### Proof

Let

\[
y=(y_0,\ldots,y_{N-1})^T\in \mathsf{Coeff}_N.
\]

After applying \(\Pi_k\), the \(r\)-th leaf receives

\[
S_ry=(y_r,y_{r+k},\ldots,y_{r+(n-1)k})^T.
\]

Interpret this vector as the coefficient vector of a small-ring polynomial

\[
Y_r(Y)=\sum_{j=0}^{n-1}y_{r+jk}Y^j.
\]

Applying \(\mathsf{S2C}_n\) to the \(r\)-th leaf produces

\[
\operatorname{can}_n(Y_r).
\]

Now merge the \(k\) leaf outputs. By the definition of \(\mathsf{Merge}_k\), the merged large-ring polynomial is

\[
Y(X)=\sum_{r=0}^{k-1}X^rY_r(X^k).
\]

Expanding this expression gives

\[
\begin{aligned}
Y(X)
&=
\sum_{r=0}^{k-1}
X^r
\left(
\sum_{j=0}^{n-1}y_{r+jk}(X^k)^j
\right)
\\
&=
\sum_{r=0}^{k-1}
\sum_{j=0}^{n-1}
y_{r+jk}X^{r+jk}.
\end{aligned}
\]

Every index \(i\in\{0,\ldots,N-1\}\) is uniquely written as \(i=r+jk\) with \(0\le r<k\) and \(0\le j<n\). Hence

\[
Y(X)=\sum_{i=0}^{N-1}y_iX^i.
\]

Therefore the leaf route outputs the canonical-slot vector of the large-ring polynomial whose coefficient vector is \(y\):

\[
\operatorname{can}_N\left(\sum_{i=0}^{N-1}y_iX^i\right).
\]

This is exactly the definition of

\[
\mathsf{S2C}_N(y).
\]

Thus

\[
\mathsf{Merge}_k
\circ
\left(\bigoplus_{r=0}^{k-1}\mathsf{S2C}_n\right)
\circ
\Pi_k
=
\mathsf{S2C}_N.
\]

\(\square\)

### 9. Ideal Normalized CKKS Core Factorization

#### Theorem 6: Ideal Normalized CKKS Core Factorization under Coefficient-Split Semantics

Assume the following normalized message-level model.

1. For \(d\in\{N,n\}\), the maps \(\operatorname{can}_d:\mathcal R_d\to\mathsf{Can}_d\) and \(\operatorname{coeff}_d:\mathcal R_d\to\mathsf{Coeff}_d\) are fixed linear coordinate isomorphisms.

2. The ideal linear transforms are

\[
\mathsf{C2S}_d
=
\operatorname{coeff}_d\circ\operatorname{can}_d^{-1},
\qquad
\mathsf{S2C}_d
=
\operatorname{can}_d\circ\operatorname{coeff}_d^{-1}.
\]

3. The maps \(\mathsf{Split}_k\) and \(\mathsf{Merge}_k\) are induced by the unique module decomposition

\[
P(X)=\sum_{r=0}^{k-1}X^rP_r(X^k).
\]

4. The layout map \(\Pi_k\) is residue-class grouping, up to fixed coordinate permutations only. Any diagonal scaling or library-specific normalization has already been absorbed into \(\mathsf{C2S}\), \(\mathsf{S2C}\), and the scalar EvalMod function.

5. The large-ring and leaf-ring EvalMod stages apply the same component-wise scalar map under the same normalization:

\[
\mathsf{EvalMod}_N=f^{\oplus N},
\qquad
\mathsf{EvalMod}_n=f^{\oplus n}.
\]

Here "same normalization" includes the same approximation polynomial, period normalization, input scaling, and output scaling.

Then the ideal normalized CKKS bootstrapping core satisfies

\[
\mathsf{S2C}_N\circ
\mathsf{EvalMod}_N\circ
\mathsf{C2S}_N
=
\mathsf{Merge}_k
\circ
\left(
\bigoplus_{r=0}^{k-1}
\mathsf{S2C}_n\circ
\mathsf{EvalMod}_n\circ
\mathsf{C2S}_n
\right)
\circ
\mathsf{Split}_k.
\]

#### Proof

This is an immediate instance of Theorem 1.

Indeed, by Lemma 3,

\[
\left(\bigoplus_r\mathsf{C2S}_n\right)\circ \mathsf{Split}_k
=
\Pi_k\circ \mathsf{C2S}_N.
\]

By Lemma 4,

\[
\left(\bigoplus_r\mathsf{EvalMod}_n\right)\circ \Pi_k
=
\Pi_k\circ \mathsf{EvalMod}_N.
\]

By Lemma 5,

\[
\mathsf{Merge}_k
\circ
\left(\bigoplus_r\mathsf{S2C}_n\right)
\circ
\Pi_k
=
\mathsf{S2C}_N.
\]

The three compatibility conditions of the split-compatible sandwich theorem are therefore satisfied with

\[
L^{(1)}=\mathsf{C2S},
\qquad
\Phi=\mathsf{EvalMod},
\qquad
L^{(2)}=\mathsf{S2C}.
\]

Hence

\[
\mathsf{S2C}_N\circ
\mathsf{EvalMod}_N\circ
\mathsf{C2S}_N
=
\mathsf{Merge}_k
\circ
\left(
\bigoplus_{r=0}^{k-1}
\mathsf{S2C}_n\circ
\mathsf{EvalMod}_n\circ
\mathsf{C2S}_n
\right)
\circ
\mathsf{Split}_k.
\]

\(\square\)

### 10. Consequence: No Slot-Recovery Transform is Needed

Theorem 6 explains why the factorization does not require a slot-preserving split. Coefficient split generally does not preserve the original large-ring canonical slots. For example, when \(k=2\) and

\[
P(X)=A(X^2)+XB(X^2),
\]

large-ring paired slots satisfy

\[
P(\alpha)=A(\theta)+\alpha B(\theta),
\qquad
P(-\alpha)=A(\theta)-\alpha B(\theta),
\]

where \(\theta=\alpha^2\). Thus the leaf slots \(A(\theta)\) and \(B(\theta)\) are local linear combinations of \(P(\alpha)\) and \(P(-\alpha)\), not selected large-ring slots.

If one wanted the leaf ciphertexts to preserve the original large-ring slot values, an additional slot-lifting or slot-recovery transform would be needed. In the \(k=2\) case, this would take the form

\[
Q^+(Y)=A(Y)+h(Y)B(Y),
\qquad
Q^-(Y)=A(Y)-h(Y)B(Y),
\]

where \(h(\theta)=\alpha\). More generally, such a transform corresponds to a fiber-wise Vandermonde map between coefficient-split coordinates and slot-preserving coordinates.

The polynomial \(h\) should not be interpreted as a canonical square-root map in the quotient ring. Over \(\mathbb C\), such an \(h\) exists by interpolation on the finite set of roots of \(Y^n+1\), after choosing one lift \(\alpha\) above each \(\theta\). This choice is branch-dependent and noncanonical. It is not part of the coefficient-split semantics used in Theorem 6.

However, CKKS bootstrapping does not require the original canonical slots immediately after the split. The EvalMod stage is applied after \(\mathsf{C2S}\), and the input to EvalMod consists of coefficient-packed slots. By Lemma 3, coefficient split followed by leaf \(\mathsf{C2S}_n\) already produces the same coefficient-packed coordinates as large-ring \(\mathsf{C2S}_N\), grouped by residue class. Therefore the slot-recovery transform is not part of the factorized bootstrapping core.

This is the mathematical reason behind the no-\(B_k\) route:

\[
\mathsf{Split}_k
\to
\bigoplus_r\mathsf{C2S}_n
\to
\bigoplus_r\mathsf{EvalMod}_n
\to
\bigoplus_r\mathsf{S2C}_n
\to
\mathsf{Merge}_k
\]

instead of

\[
\mathsf{Split}_k
\to
\text{slot recovery}
\to
\cdots.
\]

---

### 11. Ciphertext-Level Interpretation

The theorem above is a message-level algebraic statement. In a ciphertext-level construction, the ideal split and merge are implemented by ring-switching operations:

\[
\mathsf{RSDown}_{N\to n}
\quad\text{and}\quad
\mathsf{RSUp}_{n\to N}.
\]

The intended semantics are:

\[
\mathbf{ct}_N \vDash_{\Delta,N} P(X)
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
\mathsf{RSDown}_{N\to n}(\mathbf{ct}_N)
=
(\mathbf{ct}_{n,0},\ldots,\mathbf{ct}_{n,k-1}),
\]

with

\[
\mathbf{ct}_{n,r}\vDash_{\Delta,n} P_r(Y).
\]

Then each leaf executes the leaf bootstrapping core, and \(\mathsf{RSUp}_{n\to N}\) merges the outputs back to the large ring. Theorem 6 justifies the message semantics of the leaf cores. It does not by itself bound:

- key-switching error in \(\mathsf{RSDown}\) or \(\mathsf{RSUp}\);
- EvalMod approximation error;
- C2S/S2C linear transform error;
- RNS rounding and rescale error;
- implementation normalization factors;
- security of the leaf rings and evaluation keys.

These issues must be handled in the parameter, noise, and security analysis.

Equivalently, Theorem 6 proves only the ideal message-level equality. A ciphertext-level theorem must separately prove that \(\mathsf{RSDown}_{N\to n}\) implements \(\mathsf{Split}_k\) and that \(\mathsf{RSUp}_{n\to N}\) implements \(\mathsf{Merge}_k\), up to controlled error and the same normalization assumed above.

```text

```

3. 再证明 CKKS core 满足抽象定理的三个条件：

```text
Split-C2S compatibility
EvalMod component-wise compatibility

```

```text
S2C_N EvalMod_N C2S_N
=
Merge_k (oplus_r S2C_n EvalMod_n C2S_n) Split_k.
```
